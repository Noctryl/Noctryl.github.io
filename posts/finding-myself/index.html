who am i

POSTS

21 Feb 2026

Answering “who am I?” sounds simple, but it really isn’t. It’s not that I don’t have words. It’s more that every answer feels temporary, like I’m just trying on identities instead of actually finding something real. People say it’s normal to feel lost as a teenager, but I can’t help wondering… do adults actually figure it out, or do they just get better at pretending they have?

I don’t even know how self-knowledge is supposed to work. Is it just knowing what you like and dislike? Or is there something deeper, something stable underneath all of that? A friend of mine once said he knows himself well, and the way he said it, so calmly, so confidently, kind of unsettled me. I couldn’t relate to that at all. It made me wonder if he really has that clarity, or if he’s just told himself a story enough times that it started to feel true.

Sometimes it feels like people fall into three groups: those who are genuinely sure of who they are, those who act like they are, and those who can see through the act but don’t have anything solid to replace it with. I think I’m in that last group. Even my past goals, becoming an IAS officer, a computer scientist, a banker, don’t feel like they came from me. They feel like things I absorbed from the world around me. I liked the idea of them, the respect, the image, not the actual day-to-day reality.

Right now, I feel pulled toward mechanical engineering. And weirdly, it’s not because of money or status. If anything, I’ve started losing interest in those things. What draws me is something else. Machines feel honest. An engine works or it doesn’t. There’s no pretending, no performance, no identity to maintain. Just function.

But even then, I doubt myself. Is this real interest, or just attraction to the idea of speed, of Formula 1, of something intense and exciting? Because actual work isn’t like that. It’s slow, repetitive, sometimes boring. And my mind, it craves stimulation. It jumps from one thing to another. So I can’t tell if I truly like this path, or just the aesthetic of it.

If you took away everything, money, expectations, validation, what others think, I honestly don’t know what I’d choose.

And maybe that’s the part that bothers me the most.
