Why writing fiction is tough?

I used to read a lot in high school. I wouldn't call myself a book nerd, but I genuinely enjoyed reading. It felt soo cool, and I had relatively less academic stress. However, that thing completely changed since last year, all thanks to my entrance exam preparation, which is again, very rigorous. It demands a lot of time. I do have regrets for not reading books in these 2 years but again if I was in a very good position academically then I wouldn’t have thought much about it. It fucking stings when the only thing you were supposed to do properly, doesn’t go well at all, and honestly speaking, stakes are kind of high. Not that it’ll lead me to nowhere but I could literally choose the type of life I’d have spend in my undergraduate years. They say, life is full of regrets and unfinished dreams.

Honestly speaking, there was no culture of reading books in particularly my nuclear family. My family always encouraged me to read textbooks and newspapers, which helped me develop an extensive Hindi vocabulary. I could spell and write grammatically correct Hindi quite early in childhood.

I don’t know why but I’ve always felt there’s a certain amount of disdain in my parents if they see me reading. Not as in they’d snatch my books and throw them in a dustbin, but a very subtle way of expressing disappointment initially.

Let’s say if I want to get a fiction book, they’d probably agree for one, not more than that and academic textbooks must be my first priority, and other books should be read in remaining them, maybe they’re right in their place because I’ve this very bad habit of finishing a book in one continuous run.

I still remember the night vividly when I started reading One Night@Call Centre in ninth standard.

*Winters, 2024.*

*It was the chilly night of January. North Indian winters are as brutal as the summers.*

We had finished our dinner and I was busy doing maths. I was struggling in maths at that time, so it was a tough study session for me. I studied till 12 and then I went to sleep.
We have this rule in our family to sleep by 12. However, this doesn’t apply to me because I often stay up till 2 or even 3. In those days, I used to sleep very early, unlike now. Part of it was because I had the freedom to unofficially do anything I wanted at night. No one was going to check on us.

*I turned off the lights, and then went to bed, covering myself up in the thick blanket.*
*12:00 AM, the clock ticked.*

India is a very lively country in general. You’d probably never feel bore here because soo much happen every single moment. It’s a perfect country to visit if you want to overload your sensory. However, winters have this distinct ability to slow everything down to literal stillness.

*Tick, tick, tick, tick, The clock made noise loudly. It was evidently loud.*

Generally, nights aren’t this quite here because almost 10 months, fan or Air conditioners run at my place. Constant noise, and then, since my home is a kilometre away from a national highway, the rumbling of trucks is audible enough, though not that loud. Here the trucks aren’t allowed to move before 10PM in the night and after 8 in the morning, often called “no entry” here. Since it was past 10, drivers would’ve waited the whole day for this moment and why wouldn’t they? They pay the highest prices for toll taxes.

But the winters are different. Whole north India is covered with thick fog, making it literally impossible to drive because of “0 visibility” when you can’t see objects in front of you. When I went to close and lock the main gate, I saw the dominant winters myself, fog creeping through the streets, slowly swallowing the city. You can pass a breath out of your mouth and watch heavy smoke/fog like effect coming out of your mouth.
The whole city stood still that night. No sound of fans or rumbling of trucks. There was this uncomfortable *eeriness* in that night, where you could only listen to the loud, mechanical clock sounds.

I tried to sleep, but I couldn’t. *Tick, tick, tick, tick.*

I checked the time. It was 12:30 AM.

Since I had nothing to do, I swiftly opened my laptop in dark and cranked up the night mode to the yellowest. I had this book in my mind to read, but I was procrastinating to do so, hence I opened that e-book

*One Night@Call Centre,* the title said.

Suddenly, a noise came in the background
*Thduck-thduck, thduck-thduck.*

Some train was passing by. Since our home is 800 metres away from the mainline, track sounds are quite evident during winters.

Till then, my blanket had been warm enough. Now I could lay down and read it. I started it at 12:35 AM. Everyone else in my family was sleeping. The premise of the story was a group of 5 people who were lost in their life and had a miserable job, got a call from God. The plot was engaging enough, and then I lost the count of time. I was soo submerged and determined enough to finish the story. Now I can’t even sit for an hour reading textbooks ):
I finished through halfway after three hours of reading. By then I was getting sleepy, but I made myself wide awake. I drank a glass of water.

*Creak*
A loud sound came from the adjacent room. I got scared and quickly turned of my laptop and curled up to bed. I knew it was my papa, because he had this habit to go to bathroom in early morning. After he went sleeping in his room, I checked the time, it said,
6:30AM

*Damn, I had been reading for almost 6 hours straight. I couldn’t believe myself I had skip the whole night without sleeping.* It was a big deal to me because I have rarely stayed awake till late night. But that day was different. I was soo hooked of the book that I forgot to sleep. I went to sleep at 7 and woke up at 10. 3 Hours of sleep, 6 Hours of reading. I felt accomplished that I finished the book in a single read. That was my singlehandedly best reading feat ever and I was soo proud of myself.

That night, however, marked the beginning of a rather different relationship with books.

That was a particularly important day for me.

It’s been almost 3 years since that. I haven’t touched books after then, particularly in fiction or so, though I’ve read maybe 3-4 others.

Now I occasionally write on my website. I thought it’d be somewhat easy as you could just pour whatever you’re thinking in your mind, give it a shape and write it. But as I actually sat down and began to write it, it posed a great dilemma to me- What to write. It’s more of an intellectual freeze. Having the idea of story itself is a great deal but evolving a whole plot around it requires heavy mental workout. As someone said before

*“All of us who do creative work, we get into it because we have good taste. But there is this gap. For the couple first years you make stuff, it's just not that good. It's trying to be good, it has potential, but it's not…”*

While writing non-fiction, authors often have inherent boundary and structure, but when they try to explore fiction, the boundary is shattered and there are infinite possibilities to write. I could write about my preparation, my childhood, or the books I had read. But when I tried to invent something that had never happened, I suddenly had nothing to say. It often leads to the paradox of having infinite choices to write but not finding an apt subject to write it.

Having a taste for literature doesn't necessarily mean possessing the ability to write it. One can have a great taste in novels but when it comes writing first draft, it rarely matches the vivid, emotionally resonant version in your head. Bridging that gap requires turning off your internal critique long enough to produce a flawed draft that can actually be edited.

It’s worth noting that the profound creative isolation takes a huge toll on the human brain, it's not only tiring physically but psychologically. For long stretches of time, an author exists in a private reality that no one can see, validate or share. Believing that a non-existent world matters enough to spend months on requires an immense act of solitary faith, which often leads to deep self-doubt and loneliness.

Perhaps the most difficult part of writing fiction, however, is its emotional vulnerability it exposes to the reader. You can’t write a character without showing authentic human emotions. You cannot depict genuine cruelty, shame, grief, or petty jealousy without exploring your own emotional library. Writing honest fiction often feels like you are exposing your own naked emotions, closely tied to anxiety of being misinterpreted, or worse, seen accurately. They often think readers would tie the characters’ darker impulses to authors’ own morality. Writing often bypasses logic and taps into the subconscious. Sometimes as the story develops an author unknowingly incorporates their own childhood grief, trauma or wanting something they don’t really have now. Many writers sit down to write an entertaining adventure only to realize halfway through that they are actually processing an old loss or a difficult relationship that is hard to process.

Nevertheless I might try writing fiction in future because part of me still believes it’s an excellent way of self-expression or the limits of human imagination.
