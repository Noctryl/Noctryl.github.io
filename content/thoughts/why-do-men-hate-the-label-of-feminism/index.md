title: "Why Do Men Hate the Label of Feminism?"
date: 2026-10-30T20:33:00+05:30

---

*My parents never taught me about feminism. Maybe they themselves don’t know the label.*

As far as I can remember, my parents never taught me anything remotely related to feminism. It was never a thing in my household. As you can probably guess, I was born and brought up in a mildly or very slightly liberal family. We never talked about girls or women anyway, because we neither had sisters nor did it occur to my brother or me to discuss anything about them. My friend group was confined to guys only, though I did have one female friend when I was in preschool. My mom and her mom were close friends; we used to play different types of games and all.

As I grew older, my friend circle was strictly limited to guys only. Though I studied in a co-ed school, we never talked to girls or even bothered about them. We were just too busy with ourselves. So, apart from my mother, there were no female figures in my life.

Though we didn’t discuss feminism or anything in our house, our parents didn’t raise my brother and me with, I’d rather say, a patriarchal mindset. We were told to do everything by ourselves and were never taught that something was meant for girls or boys. I’d say we were raised pretty much like a blank slate, without having any strong interpretation of either side, which later on turned out to be a good thing for me.

Since my childhood, I've had a particularly strong disdain for people who are sexist or say something misogynistic. Though I'd like to conclude all of this under an umbrella term because there's a lot more to cover here, the basic idea is that I never really liked people who talked badly about women in any way, and this was happening when I wasn't raised in a household where we talked about any of this. It was on me to figure it out.

I started noticing it through subtle things.

One day, one of my friends' moms told her not to go out after 8 PM because it'd be unsafe. *Unsafe? From whom?*

I was allowed to go outside. When I asked her mom why she wasn't allowed to go outside, she said it wasn't safe for girls. *Safety from whom?*

It captured my mind for some time. I was 11 at that point, without that much thinking ability, but it kept me thinking... Why are we treating girls and boys differently? Why is society not blaming bad people but women? And it's kind of a rabbit hole that would never end if I even started talking about it. Her mother never gave me a satisfactory explanation, after all. And questioning our elders isn't a thing in our society and is frowned upon. So I was on my own to figure it out.

I then started reading more about it. About how women are marginalised in every aspect of society. About how patriarchy is, sadly, commonly institutionalised in our society, and the unwillingness of our governments to make things better. I've been furious at the government at times, but when I saw the same misogynistic people holding top bureaucratic and governmental positions, I kind of lost my faith in the system, and that faith hasn't been recovered yet.

Eventually, I found myself identifying with feminism. It wasn't something I had inherited from my family or something I had been formally introduced to at school. It was a perspective I gradually developed through observation, conversations and independent reading. But that also made me curious about something else. If the fundamental principles of feminism are equality and dignity, why does the label itself generate so much resistance, particularly among men? Why are there men who might agree with its underlying principles but still hesitate to associate themselves with it?

Environment plays a big role because humans can be easily influenced or manipulated. Human brains act according to the situation, rewards and the social setting, and when your environment applauds you for saying something sexist or misogynistic, it doesn't help at all. Luckily, all my close friends throughout the years were a bunch of genuinely smart and respectful people towards anybody. And it's kind of important because, well, because most of the classrooms are heavily misogynistic, and people actually think it's cool to make comments like these.

Long story short, it's a huge issue here. And I've seen people who'll pretend to be feminists in order to get approval for "being the nice and good guy" while having a deeply sickening mindset from within. This might make another topic of pretentious feminism, but both of them closely follow each other. Because people do not want to get themselves associated with the label of feminism. Okay, there's a very large section of society that outright dismisses every point of feminism and continues to be patriarchal, but even among those who seem to support the whole thing, many are very sceptical of the label of feminist itself.

Maybe this is the collective failure of our society itself because, well, obviously, it was never presented to us as something that promotes equality but rather as something evil done by women to disrupt the world order. And the conditioning is such that people actually think it's a term coined by women, for women, and that if they'd accept it, they'd become more feminine – and, of course, less masculine. And society has never really applauded men who openly embraced feminism. It was always something to be looked down upon. And then there's this social media sphere, where everyone is trying to sell their fake, cheap ideology in order to actually fit in.

In my humble opinion, if society won't make it publicly acceptable for men to embrace feminism and openly condemn patriarchal ideas, it isn't going anywhere soon. Boys are often told to be strong and not to cry from childhood, and feminism is something considered feminine. If you're a feminist – boom, you don't have a spine, you're girly and whatnot??

And humans are pretty much social animals.

They want validation, acceptance and social status from society. If our society won't make these basic things acceptable and stop shaming men for embracing feminism, it won't go anywhere.

Though it would be unfair to call out individual behaviour too. Often, people make misogynistic comments under the guise of worrying about what society will think of them. It's a negative feedback loop. And feminism's linguistic framing itself bothers some people. But that shouldn't be a reason to reject something that's good for all people, though…

*Just a thought.*
