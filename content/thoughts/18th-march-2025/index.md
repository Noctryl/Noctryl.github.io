1st of April, 2025.

“Why the fuck am I here?” I thought as I was trying to understand what work function and de Broglie wavelength were.

“How the fuck do these people know what the 40th element of the periodic table is on the first day of coaching?”

I was heavily regretting my life choices. I know it’d be too early to judge, but I felt like a misfit there: a class of 60 students, one large room, that greyish, depressing light, and our chemistry teacher. My mind thought of every fucking possibility to get out of that classroom. I desperately waited for the class to end.

The minutes crawled, each one reminding me that I was somewhere I didn't quite belong.

18th March, 2025.

10:30 AM

The bell rang, indicating that we should start writing the paper. I read the paper during the allocated time. My initial impressions were that the paper was doable enough. I started writing then. I wrote frantically for the first 30 minutes, unfazed, with full concentration, in a state of flow. After 30 minutes, I kind of felt tired of writing. I remember it was the season of spring. I was wearing my full-sleeved school shirt. The fans were off because the temperature wasn’t that high, and North Indian springs are surprisingly pleasant.

A breeze of wind stroked my cheeks, my hair waving. I didn’t have a haircut for a month or two because all I did at home was study or waste time on random rabbit holes.

I looked outside the classroom through the window. It was still outside, as if everything in nature had been turned to rock. I felt relieved that, finally, my Class 10 was going to end. However, this same thought scared me because I would then have to change schools in Class 11: a completely new environment, new people, and a new course. I was confused about what to feel. Anyway, I went back to my paper and wrote answers in short bursts of 20 minutes for the next hour.

30 minutes were left when I finished my paper. I tried sleeping, but I couldn’t. I had always slept after my exams before, but that day was very different from the others. I thought about my future.

I was pretty much sure that I’d pursue engineering since Class 8. I researched thoroughly about it. People generally start preparation in Class 11. That time finally came. I somehow always convinced myself that classes before Class 11 didn’t matter a lot. It was sort of my coping mechanism for the occasional average marks I scored; other times, I got either the second or third rank in my class. Now was my most important phase of life. I knew that if I didn’t study hard, get disciplined enough, and stay focused, I wouldn’t make it. The stakes were high.

BRRRRRRRYNG!! The bell rang, slicing through my inner thoughts. The moment came. My board examinations finally ended.

A collective sigh ran throughout the class. Everyone seemed either relieved or happy, but I wasn’t. I kept quiet.

“How was your paper?” Diksha asked me.

“It was good,” I replied dryly. I wasn’t interested in any conversation. However, I thought it’d be unfair to be sad, as I’d see many of my friends for the last time, so I tried not to look too far into the future. I chatted with many people till I reached the exit gate. The exit was packed. Every parent was looking for their child. I went through the crowd and found my father.

My father picked me up after my exam ended.

“You’re not saying anything. Did the paper go badly?”

“Nope, the paper was good,” I said to my father.

“Then why are you so quiet now?”

“Nothing.”

I didn’t know how to explain that feeling to my father.

Nonetheless, I was mostly satisfied with my exams because I got marks according to the effort I put in. I had to cram all the useless database and Excel stuff a day or two before the exam. We had an eight-day gap between exams. Considering the fact that I barely studied for the exams throughout the year, they went absolutely well relative to my preparation. The whole year, I was either playing games in my computer lab or chatting with my friends. I still sometimes reminisce about those days.

It was the peak working hour of the day, when everyone was commuting either to their homes or offices. Roads were packed; traffic was everywhere. The sun was bright enough to make me cover up with the scarf I was carrying. I had very mixed feelings at that time. I anticipated that a whole big world was going to be exposed to me. I had to deal with almost everything by myself now.

I observed the road. Big coaching billboards boasted about their top rankers. My father stopped the bike because there was a red light ahead. Till the 30th second, a lot of bikes stood still. I felt like such a commoner that day: everyone had their own struggles; they were the main characters of their own lives, with ambitions, struggles, and whatnot. Suddenly, my own internal problems softened. I looked at the roads we were passing because there was a possibility that I’d never travel through those roads again. Funny enough, I used to hate those roads when I saw them at 7 am while coming to school. Human conscience is one hell of a thing.

As we reached the Shastri Bridge, which is almost 4 kilometres long and connects the whole city to my place, cutting through the rivers of Ganga and Yamuna, two of the sacred rivers, a slightly cold river breeze brushed through my cheeks. I felt my mixed feelings were drying up. I felt relieved because now I could happily spend two weeks doing what I wanted. I had no academic pressure or commitments. I felt like a free bird, flying in the bright blue sky, except for the fact that free birds knew where to go. I knew I could read novels, sleep late, and wake up early. Hell, I could watch movies, series, and anime all day, and no one would interrupt me.

I was about to reach home.

I slept for four hours in the evening that day. Though I don’t remember what I did later that day, it was one of the best days of my year.

I still sometimes want to go back to that day and rewrite all my past…

Because, except for a thing or two I did after that day, everything went drastically wrong with me.
