It’s the first of April, 2025.

I had my first JEE class today, yes, the moment I’ve been waiting for this exact moment for I don’t know how long. Now finally I could study the subjects I liked. I was enthusiastic about my first day of coaching but a little cautious too. I’ve heard it’s tough, difficult, and being behind is a canon event, though I thought I was different.

The day before, we got a message from our insti that our classes were scheduled from 10:40 to 2:20 PM. Alright, fair enough. Not that much.

I woke up at 8 AM in the morning. Though I used to wake up at 5 for school, 8 still felt early enough. My mom gave me 100 Rs. It took only 50 for the round trip but she gave me 50 extra, for any other adverse circumstances.

My mom was extra precarious because I’ve never travelled on my own before. I was always accompanied by my parents and I have never dealt with any real-life scenarios.

Both mom and father (I don’t like calling him dad) were instructing me on how to deal with auto drivers, where to change autos yada...

I wasn’t paying much attention to it, completely clouded by the thoughts of how it was going to be in the coaching. I’ve heard from one of my seniors that teachers are amazing, they engage with the students and try to solve their doubts.

At 9:15 AM, I left my home. I took blessings from my family and departed from home. My mother waved me bye until I was out of her sight.

I walked for 300 metres, hoping to find some autos but there were none in my sight. I continued walking for another 5 minutes until an auto stopped beside me. An uncle, probably in his early 40s asked me “Alopi Bagh?” I nodded and then he signalled me to sit. It was uncomfortable because some random guy was taking up 40% of the total space. I didn’t say anything to him because I didn’t know if he’d start a fight with me.

The next 20 minutes were full of chaos. People were stopping at arbitrary points, honking full-on, and changing lanes dangerously. It was horrifying for me, because previously I used to travel by bus for school and it didn’t bother me much, but since I was sitting in front of an auto, I could see things going literally in front of me. My brain instantly went to fight or flight mode. It was bizarre. My auto driver had near-miss crashes at least thrice during the whole trip.

I got off the auto, searching for my next ride to civil lines. It was pure chaos, honking, shouting and a lot of commotion. Auto drivers were asking me “kidhar jana hai??” again and again, and some of them even tried to shove me into their autos saying “chaliye bhaiya hum le chalenge aapko George down”. Initially I was polite and just moved my way but it was getting more irritating so I kinda said in a higher tone “nahi jaana hai yaar”. I felt bad but that was necessary. After my struggles, I finally found one. Another 20 minutes of trip down the drain.

The driver stopped at Hanuman Mandir Chauraha. He said “sirf idhar tak hi auto jaata hai” but I clearly saw autos going till the next chauraha, which was 900 metres away. I didn’t know much so I paid him and left. I had yet to cover almost a kilometre and I was already getting late for my coaching. I was walking in a hurry, concurrently asking every auto driver in that lane if they could drop me. Finally, one of them agreed, for 20 Rs.

“20? It’s only 700 metres from here?” I said.

“It is what the prices are”

I agreed to it because I had no idea whatsoever. At 10:30, I finally reached my coaching.

When I entered the room, I was horror-struck. There were 80 students in a tiny room. It had distinct characteristics. The room was devoid of windows and sunlight. It had those depressing white lights that were placed perfectly to make it even more depressing. The benches were completely packed.

The room was full of books and notebooks. I hadn’t even started the prep yet, and these people were already submerged.

I found my friend Prashant, who was sitting in the last bench, with whom I’d been friends since class 8. It was our first day in class 11th. Everyone seemed so excited about JEE preparation, asking each other about books, teacher recommendations, and strategy plans. No one knew what was coming to them in the next few months.

Suddenly, the whole class stood up in unison. Our physics teacher, a 5’11” tall guy with almond-coloured eyes, possibly in his mid-30s, entered the class. He looked at the class with his enthusiasm-filled eyes, but I wasn’t excited at all. Everything felt dull, repetitive. Maybe it was the fatigue from Class 10, or chronically researching too much about JEE prep on Reddit. Those Reddit comments only tanked all my motivation to start jee. It was bad, I should’ve been much more optimistic about my initial days in Class 11.

The class was heavily packed with students. There were 55 boys and 7 girls in our class. quite a fucked-up gender ratio. I saw many people from my own school who, along with me, had taken dummy school. I could recognize a few faces. Suddenly, I didn’t feel very lonely, even though I was sitting with my friends. I tried to analyse everyone sitting in the class. It was too early to judge someone for their intelligence or how smart they were. It turned out that I was very bad at judging people's intelligence. How would you judge someone’s performance in studies? Either they get good marks or answer questions in class. In reality, it was much more complex and nuanced. I tried focusing on the class. Harun sir was scribbling some random gibberish on the board, discussing the paper pattern, but I paid attention to none of it because I was already heavily self-educated on every aspect of jee prep, or this is what I believed in. 100 mins passed by, and it didn’t feel too long. It was time for maths period.

A tall guy with a considerably big stomach entered our class, his tummy visible two seconds before he fully entered our class. I got a sudden ick from him—not knowing what it was, because I didn’t even know this guy. There was something very much repulsive about this guy.

“Students, we’re going to start with sets.”

What the fuck, dude comes in and gets straight to teaching on the very first day, I thought.

I found his voice irritating and icky, as well as the way he was annotating on the board. He couldn’t explain the concepts to us well.

The class was over after 100 mins. 100 long minutes of my life. In all those 100 mins, the whole room smelt like despair.

The FIRST day was over. I felt relieved that it was over. Not a very good thing to say honestly, because you’re not supposed to hate the place that might actually build the foundation for your future. I felt pathetic. I asked others about their opinions on teachers, to lessen my own self-loathing. Some of them said, “teachers are okay Ish; you got to do it on your own.”

I wandered on the streets for some time then returned to my home.
